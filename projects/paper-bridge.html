Build a Small Structure

Test how folds and geometry influence the stiffness of a paper bridge.

The idea

A flat sheet is easy to bend, but folding it into a channel or corrugated form changes the shape of its cross-section. By comparing structures made from the same material, we can explore why engineers care about geometry as well as material choice.

Read the step-by-step video text
  1. Set the supports: Position two sturdy books with a 20 cm gap.
  2. Test flat paper: Place one sheet across the gap. Observe its initial shape.
  3. Add small loads: Add coins gently and record the deflection or failure point.
  4. Fold a channel: Fold an identical sheet to form a U-shaped or accordion profile.
  5. Retest: Apply the same loads at the same position and compare.
  6. Compare results: Observe how cross-section changes bending stiffness.

What you need

  1. Two sturdy books — To act as supports.
  2. Three or more identical sheets of paper — For comparing different bridge shapes.
  3. One ruler — To keep the unsupported span constant.
  4. A small collection of coins — Use as light, repeatable test weights.

Safety: Test above a table and away from fragile objects. This is not a load-bearing structure for people or valuables.

Build it

  1. Set two books 20 cm apart as supports. Lay one flat sheet of paper across the gap.
  2. Place coins gently at the centre, adding them one by one. Record the maximum number before the paper noticeably sags or fails.
  3. Make a second bridge by folding an identical sheet into an accordion or U-shaped channel.
  4. Test the folded version over the same span with the same coins, in the same order and location.

Measure and explain

Compare mid-span deflection under the same load and note the number of coins supported. For an ideal simply supported beam with a central point force, elastic beam theory gives δ = PL³/(48EI), where E is elastic modulus and I depends on cross-sectional geometry. Thin paper may crease or buckle, so this equation is a conceptual model, not an accurate prediction of your paper bridge’s failure load.

Experiment further

Change support spacing while keeping the folded shape the same. Does deflection increase? Investigate why real bridges use trusses and deep girders. Sketch the load path from the coin through the paper into the books.

CAD Design Project

Model a simple crank-slider assembly in Fusion 360 or similar CAD software.

The idea

Computer-aided design helps turn a mechanism into parts that fit together, move as intended and can be revised without starting from scratch. Here the goal is a simple crank-slider: rotary motion from a crank drives a slider back and forth along a straight guide.

Read the step-by-step video text
  1. Start the design: In Fusion 360, open the Design workspace and select the Solid tab.
  2. Create a sketch: Choose Create Sketch and select an origin plane such as XY.
  3. Sketch geometry: Use rectangle and circle tools; apply dimensions and constraints.
  4. Make solids: Finish Sketch, then Solid > Create > Extrude selected profiles.
  5. Assemble components: Create separate components. Use Assemble > Joint for revolute and slider joints.
  6. Check the movement: Drive a joint and compare extreme slider positions; inspect interferences.

What you need

  1. One computer — Able to run your chosen CAD tool.
  2. Parametric CAD software — Fusion 360 or a suitable alternative.

Safety: Check the software’s current free-use eligibility and feature restrictions. No fabrication is required.

Build it

  1. Create a base with a straight slider guide and a fixed crank pivot.
  2. Sketch and model a 20 mm crank radius and an 80 mm connecting rod; give parts clearance at pin joints.
  3. Make the crank, rod, slider and base separate components.
  4. Apply revolute joints at the pins and a slider joint along the guide. Ground the base.
  5. Drive the crank through one revolution, watching for over-constraints and interferences. Save views of the two extreme slider positions.

Measure and explain

For an ideal, in-line crank-slider, the stroke is 2r, where r is crank radius. A 20 mm crank therefore gives a nominal 40 mm stroke. Connecting-rod length affects the relationship between crank angle and slider position, even though it does not change this ideal stroke when the mechanism is configured correctly.

Experiment further

Plot slider position at crank angles 0°, 45°, 90°, 135° and 180°. Compare it with a simple sine wave. What changes if you shorten the rod? Export a drawing and check whether your design has sensible assembly tolerances.

Torque and Rotational Motion

Why does pushing a door near its hinge barely turn it?

Why forces cause turning

Torque measures a force’s turning tendency about an axis. The same force produces more torque when applied farther from the pivot and perpendicular to the lever arm. Rotational acceleration also depends on how mass is distributed around the axis.

A door opens more easily when you push far from its hinges because the same force has a greater turning effect. Torque depends on the applied force and its perpendicular distance from the pivot. This helps explain why tools have handles and why supports on a cantilever beam experience significant moments.

Translation and rotation can occur together. A freely moving rigid body can accelerate its centre of mass while also spinning. Choosing the point about which you take moments can simplify calculations, but it does not change what actually happens.

Moment arms and rotational motion

Torque magnitude is rF sin θ. For rotation about a fixed axis, net torque equals moment of inertia times angular acceleration, analogous to F = ma. A larger moment of inertia makes angular acceleration harder to achieve.

τ = rF sin θ; Στ = Iα

The torque magnitude about a chosen axis is τ=rFsinθ, where θ is the angle between the position vector and force. In a simple fixed-axis model, net torque is Iα, where I is rotational inertia about that axis and α is angular acceleration. Mass alone is not enough to predict rotational response; how mass is distributed relative to the axis also matters.

Two equal and opposite parallel forces applied at different points can produce zero net force but a nonzero couple moment. This is why checking only ΣF=0 is not sufficient for static equilibrium. For a wrench 0.25 m long with a 40 N perpendicular push, the torque magnitude is 10 N·m; redirecting the force along the handle makes the torque about the bolt nearly zero.

Worked examples

Question. A perpendicular 15 N push acts 0.80 m from a hinge. What is the torque?

Solution, step by step. θ = 90°, so sin θ = 1. Torque = 0.80 × 15 = 12 N·m. A force applied at 0.40 m would provide only 6 N·m.

Worked example 2

Question. A 20 N perpendicular force is applied 0.3 m from a hinge. Find torque magnitude.

Solution. τ=rF sin90°=0.3×20=6 N·m.

Practice set

Five questions, from quick checks to applied problems. Reveal the answer after trying each one.

Problem 1 of 5

Your turn. What torque does a 10 N perpendicular force produce at 0.25 m from a pivot?

Show worked answer

τ = 0.25 × 10 = 2.5 N·m.

Problem 2 of 5

Find torque for 10 N at 0.5 m perpendicular lever arm.

Show answer and method

5 N·m.

Problem 3 of 5

What is torque if the force acts directly through the pivot?

Show answer and method

Zero.

Problem 4 of 5

What is the SI unit of angular acceleration?

Show answer and method

rad/s².

Problem 5 of 5

A rigid body is in rotational equilibrium when…?

Show answer and method

Sum of external torques about any fixed point is zero.

Try it yourself

Use a ruler pivoted at one end and apply equal gentle forces at two distances. Compare how easily it turns.

Where this model stops

Torque is calculated about a chosen axis; a different pivot can change individual torque values.

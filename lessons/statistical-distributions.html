Statistical Distributions

Why do repeated measurements cluster around some values and not others?

Why measurements form patterns

A distribution describes which values are common, rare or impossible. Its centre and spread answer different questions: an average tells us where data cluster, while the standard deviation tells us how widely they scatter.

A collection of measurements is rarely described well by a single average. Two production lines may produce the same mean dimension but have very different spreads, which changes how many parts meet tolerance. A statistical distribution describes the overall pattern—centre, spread, asymmetry and unusual values.

Begin with a histogram of real measurements and ask what the shape suggests. Is there one central cluster? Is the distribution skewed? Are there distinct groups that might represent different machines or batches? A beautiful bell curve should not be imposed simply because it is familiar.

Spread, probability and distributions

The mean is the total divided by the number of observations. For sample standard deviation, square each deviation from the mean, sum them, divide by n − 1, then take the square root. A histogram helps reveal skew or multiple clusters.

sample mean = Σx/n; sample s = √(Σ(x−x̄)²/(n−1))

The mean measures a numerical centre, while the standard deviation measures typical spread around the mean. For a normal distribution, about 68% of values lie within one standard deviation of the mean and about 95% within two. Those percentages do not automatically apply to skewed, multimodal or otherwise non-normal data.

A probability distribution also describes what outcomes might be expected before sampling. Distinguish the population's underlying variability from uncertainty in an estimated mean, which usually decreases as independent sample size grows. For process capability, a stable process and a justified distributional model are prerequisites; a capability index alone cannot prove that a process is controlled.

Worked examples

Question. A set of measurements is 9, 10 and 11 mm. Find its mean and sample standard deviation.

Solution, step by step. Mean = (9 + 10 + 11)/3 = 10 mm. Squared deviations are 1, 0 and 1. Sample variance = 2/(3−1) = 1 mm²; s = 1 mm.

Worked example 2

Question. For data 2,4,4,6,9, calculate mean and median.

Solution. Sum=25, n=5, so mean=5. Ordered middle value is 4, so median=4.

Practice set

Five questions, from quick checks to applied problems. Reveal the answer after trying each one.

Problem 1 of 5

Your turn. Find the mean of measurements 4, 5, 6, 5.

Show worked answer

Sum = 20; divide by 4 to get a mean of 5.

Problem 2 of 5

Find mean of 3,6,9.

Show answer and method

6.

Problem 3 of 5

Find population variance of 1,3.

Show answer and method

Mean 2; variance=((1−2)²+(3−2)²)/2=1.

Problem 4 of 5

For a binomial(n=10,p=0.2), find expected successes.

Show answer and method

np=2.

Problem 5 of 5

What is the area under a probability density curve?

Show answer and method

1 over its full domain.

Try it yourself

Measure the length of the same object with a ruler repeatedly and graph your readings. Can you distinguish measurement scatter from resolution?

Where this model stops

A familiar bell curve is not a universal assumption. Inspect the observed distribution before fitting a normal model.

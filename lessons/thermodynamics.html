Introduction to Thermodynamics

How is thermal energy different from temperature and work?

Why heat and work are linked

Temperature indicates thermal state, while internal energy is energy stored microscopically within a system. Heat and work are transfers across a system boundary, not materials contained inside an object. The first law accounts for these transfers.

Thermodynamics tracks energy and its transformations at the system level. A kettle receives electrical work, warms water and loses some heat to the surroundings. We can account for these transfers without calculating the microscopic movement of every water molecule.

The first law expresses conservation of energy; the second law explains why energy conversions have direction and limits. Heat naturally flows from hotter to colder bodies without external work, and a real heat engine cannot turn all incoming heat into useful work. These are constraints on what machines can achieve, not merely rules for solving textbook equations.

Energy balance and thermodynamic systems

For a closed system with negligible motion and elevation effects, internal-energy change equals heat added minus work done by the system. If a gas is heated in a fixed-volume rigid container, boundary work is approximately zero.

ΔU = Q − W (W = work done by the system)

For a closed system, a common energy balance is ΔU=Q−W, with Q defined as heat entering and W as work done by the system. Sign conventions vary between texts, so always state yours. Enthalpy is particularly useful for flowing systems, where fluid carries internal energy and flow work through an inlet or outlet.

Imagine a well-insulated rigid container heated electrically. Because the boundary does not move, boundary work is zero; with negligible other energy exchanges, the increase in internal energy equals the electrical energy supplied. Temperature change depends on the substance and its heat capacity. The ideal gas law and simple constant-heat-capacity relations are useful approximations, but they have operating limits.

Worked examples

Question. A closed system receives 500 J of heat and does 120 J of work on its surroundings. Find ΔU.

Solution, step by step. Using the stated sign convention, ΔU = 500 − 120 = +380 J. The system’s internal energy rises.

Worked example 2

Question. A gas absorbs 500 J of heat and does 180 J of work on its surroundings. Find ΔU using ΔU=Q−W.

Solution. ΔU=500−180=320 J; positive means internal energy increases.

Practice set

Five questions, from quick checks to applied problems. Reveal the answer after trying each one.

Problem 1 of 5

Your turn. A system loses 200 J as heat while 50 J of work is done on it. Find ΔU.

Show worked answer

Q = −200 J and W = −50 J (work on system). ΔU = −200 − (−50) = −150 J.

Problem 2 of 5

For Q=100 J into a system and W=0, ΔU?

Show answer and method

100 J.

Problem 3 of 5

An isolated system has what heat and work interactions?

Show answer and method

No heat transfer and no work transfer across its boundary.

Problem 4 of 5

Why does temperature alone not determine heat transferred?

Show answer and method

Heat depends on the process and amount/property of matter, not temperature alone.

Problem 5 of 5

In an ideal gas at fixed n and T, if volume doubles, pressure becomes…?

Show answer and method

Half, by pV=nRT.

Try it yourself

Draw a box representing a kettle of water. Trace where energy enters and leaves, including the heater, steam and air.

Where this model stops

State the system boundary and sign convention first. Open systems also transport energy with flowing mass.

Matrices and Linear Equations

How can several equations be solved as one organised problem?

Organising many equations at once

A matrix stores coefficients in rows and columns. This is useful whenever several unknowns influence several observations, such as balancing structural forces or combining data measurements. Matrix operations preserve patterns that become hard to see in long equations.

A matrix is a structured way to keep track of many related numbers. Imagine three unknown currents in an electrical circuit or three unknown forces in an equilibrium problem. Rather than writing and rearranging each equation separately, we can record their coefficients together and apply systematic operations.

Matrices are also transformation tools. A rotation takes a coordinate pair and gives another pair; a matrix describes the rule in one compact object. That is the connection to CAD, graphics, robotics and machine learning: many quantities can be transformed or compared together without losing their relationships.

Representing linear systems with matrices

Two equations x + y = 5 and 2x − y = 1 can be represented by a coefficient matrix A, unknown vector x and outcome vector b. The compact notation Ax = b is not a new law; it is another way to write the simultaneous equations.

A = [[1,1],[2,−1]]; Ax = [5,1]ᵀ

For two linear equations, 2x+y=5 and x−y=1, write A=[[2,1],[1,−1]], the unknown vector u=[x,y] and the right-hand side b=[5,1]. The equation Au=b encodes both equations at once. Adding equations or performing row operations lets us solve for x=2 and y=1.

The determinant offers clues about whether a square system has a unique solution, but it is not a universal test for every rectangular system. Two equations that describe the same line do not uniquely determine two variables. In practical computation, solving Au=b is usually preferable to explicitly calculating an inverse; numerical methods can be more stable and efficient.

Worked examples

Question. Solve x + y = 5 and 2x − y = 1.

Solution, step by step. Add the equations to eliminate y: 3x = 6, so x = 2. Substitute back: y = 5 − 2 = 3. The matrix represents the same steps systematically.

Worked example 2

Question. Solve x+y=5 and x−y=1 using elimination.

Solution. Add equations to get 2x=6 so x=3. Substitute into x+y=5 gives y=2.

Practice set

Your turn. Solve x + y = 7 and x − y = 1.

Show worked answer

Adding gives 2x = 8, so x = 4 and y = 3.

Problem 2 of 5

Add matrices [[1,2],[3,4]] and [[2,0],[−1,5]].

Show answer and method

[[3,2],[2,9]].

Problem 3 of 5

Multiply [[1,2],[0,1]] by vector [3,4].

Show answer and method

[11,4].

Problem 4 of 5

Find det([[2,1],[3,4]]).

Show answer and method

2×4−1×3=5.

Problem 5 of 5

When does a square matrix lack an inverse?

Show answer and method

When its determinant is zero.

Try it yourself

Arrange measured costs of two parts in a 2×2 table; explore what happens to your equations if the rows become identical.

Where this model stops

If equations duplicate one another, the coefficient matrix is singular and a unique solution may not exist.

Moles and Chemical Calculations

How do chemists count enormous numbers of atoms using an ordinary balance?

Counting particles without counting individually

The mole is a counting unit, analogous to a dozen but vastly larger. One mole contains exactly 6.02214076 × 10²³ specified entities. Molar mass connects that microscopic count to mass measured in the laboratory.

A mole is a convenient counting unit for unimaginably large numbers of particles. Just as a dozen means twelve objects, one mole means exactly 6.02214076×10²³ specified entities. We can weigh a sample rather than attempting to count its molecules one by one, because molar mass links mass and amount of substance.

This matters whenever reactants are mixed. Chemical reactions occur through particle-number ratios, while a laboratory balance gives mass. Converting between grams and moles connects the world of measured materials to the balanced chemical equation.

Moles, mass and proportions

For a pure compound, amount in moles equals mass divided by molar mass. Use the chemical formula to calculate molar mass from atomic masses, then scale the reaction equation in mole ratios.

n = m/M; N = nNₐ

The relationship n=m/M uses amount n in moles, mass m and molar mass M in compatible units. Water has molar mass about 18.015 g/mol, so 36.03 g of water is approximately 2.00 mol. That represents about 1.20×10²⁴ water molecules.

In stoichiometry, convert each available reactant to moles, use coefficients from the balanced equation, identify any limiting reactant, and then calculate the predicted product. A large mass does not necessarily mean a large number of moles: molar mass differs by substance. The actual yield may be lower because reactions can be incomplete or competing processes may occur.

Worked examples

Question. How many moles are in 36 g of water? Use M(H₂O) ≈ 18 g/mol.

Solution, step by step. n = m/M = 36/18 = 2 mol. This corresponds to about 2 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 1.2044 × 10²⁴ water molecules.

Worked example 2

Question. How many moles are in 18 g water if M(H₂O)=18 g/mol? Roughly how many molecules?

Solution. n=m/M=18/18=1 mol. Number ≈6.022×10²³ molecules.

Practice set

Five questions, from quick checks to applied problems. Reveal the answer after trying each one.

Problem 1 of 5

Your turn. How many moles are in 44 g of CO₂, taking molar mass as 44 g/mol?

Show worked answer

n = 44/44 = 1 mol.

Problem 2 of 5

Find the mass of 0.25 mol of CO₂ (molar mass 44 g/mol).

Show answer and method

m = nM = 0.25 × 44 = 11 g.

Problem 3 of 5

Mass of 0.5 mol NaCl at 58.5 g/mol?

Show answer and method

29.25 g.

Problem 4 of 5

Molecules in 2 mol using Avogadro constant 6.022×10²³/mol?

Show answer and method

1.2044×10²⁴ molecules.

Problem 5 of 5

Is a mole a measure of mass or amount of substance?

Show answer and method

Amount of substance.

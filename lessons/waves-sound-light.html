Waves, Sound and Light

What travels when a ripple crosses a pond?

A disturbance that moves through space

A wave carries a disturbance and energy through space or a medium, often without transporting the medium itself over long distances. Frequency counts cycles per second; wavelength measures the distance between repeating points. Their product gives propagation speed.

A wave transports a disturbance and energy from place to place without requiring the medium itself to travel the whole distance. A pulse on a rope makes individual points move locally, while the pulse travels along the rope. Sound needs a material medium; light can propagate through vacuum.

The same broad ideas—amplitude, wavelength, frequency and superposition—appear in acoustics, optics, vibration measurement and radio communications. But different waves have different physical mechanisms, so a sound wave in air should not be imagined as exactly the same kind of oscillation as an electromagnetic wave.

Wavelength, frequency and wave speed

For a periodic travelling wave, speed = frequency × wavelength. Sound in air is a mechanical pressure wave; light is an electromagnetic wave and can travel through vacuum. Waves reflect, interfere and sometimes form standing patterns.

v = fλ

Wave speed is v=fλ, where f is frequency and λ wavelength in the medium. If a sound wave travels at roughly 343 m/s and has frequency 686 Hz, its wavelength is about 0.5 m. Changes in medium can change wave speed and wavelength while frequency often stays set by the source at a boundary.

When waves overlap, their displacements combine; this produces interference. Reflection can create standing waves, which explain resonances in pipes and strings. For light, refraction occurs when propagation speed changes between media; the ray direction often changes too. A numerical formula is useful only after identifying which wave and medium are involved.

Worked examples

Question. A sound wave travels at 340 m/s and has frequency 170 Hz. Find wavelength.

Solution, step by step. λ = v/f = 340/170 = 2 m. Doubling frequency at the same wave speed would halve wavelength.

Worked example 2

Question. A wave travels at 340 m/s with frequency 170 Hz. Find wavelength.

Solution. v=fλ, so λ=340/170=2 m.

Practice set

Five questions, from quick checks to applied problems. Reveal the answer after trying each one.

Problem 1 of 5

Your turn. A ripple has wavelength 0.25 m and frequency 4 Hz. Find propagation speed.

Show worked answer

v = 4 × 0.25 = 1 m/s.

Problem 2 of 5

Frequency 5 Hz means how many cycles per second?

Show answer and method

5.

Problem 3 of 5

A wave has λ=0.5 m and f=20 Hz. Speed?

Show answer and method

10 m/s.

Problem 4 of 5

What changes during reflection from an ideal stationary mirror: light frequency or direction?

Show answer and method

Direction changes; frequency remains unchanged.

Problem 5 of 5

Can a sound wave propagate through vacuum?

Show answer and method

No; sound requires a material medium.

Try it yourself

Move one end of a rope gently to send a pulse. Observe whether a marker on the rope travels with the pulse.

Where this model stops

Wave speed depends on the medium and conditions; do not use the speed of light for sound waves.
